Australia's
Jobs Divide

Where do Australians live — and where is the pressure to find work most intense? This visualisation maps the relationship between population distribution and job-seeker concentration across all 88 statistical regions of Australia, revealing a nation of striking contrasts between booming cities, slower-growing coastal towns, and remote communities.

Part One

Where Australians Live

Australia's population clusters almost entirely along the eastern seaboard and south-western corner. New South Wales and Victoria alone are home to more than half of all Australians, while the vast interior remains sparsely populated.

Population by State, June 2025
Source: ABS Regional Population Growth, 2024–25. NSW leads with 8.6 million residents. The NT is home to fewer than 265,000 people — less than 1% of the national total.
Annual Population Growth by State, 2024–25
Western Australia grew fastest at 2.2%. Tasmania recorded the slowest growth at 0.3%.
Components of Population Change by State
Three ABS columns — natural increase, net internal migration, and net overseas migration — combined into one stacked bar per state. QLD and WA are the only states gaining residents internally.

💡 NSW loses around 24,000 residents per year to other states, yet still grows — powered by over 90,000 overseas arrivals annually. Queensland gains from both internal migration and overseas arrivals.

Part Two

The Search for Work

Job seeker registrations have grown from 11.9 million in 2015 to 14.6 million in 2026 — a rise of 22.5%. The pressure is distributed unevenly across regions, occupations, and states.

Job Seekers per 1,000 People by SA4 Region
Each SA4 region is shaded by the number of job seekers per 1,000 residents. Darker red indicates greater pressure. Boundary data from ABS SA4 Digital Boundary Files, ASGS Edition 3 (2021). Hover over any region for details.
National Job Seeker Registrations, September 2015 – March 2026
Registrations plateaued briefly before the COVID-19 period (shaded), then accelerated from 2021 onwards.
Job Seeker Trend by State, 2015–2025
Five states shown on a shared Y-axis scale for direct comparison. Queensland's steep upward trend reflects both population growth and its expanding labour market.
Top 15 Occupations by Job Seeker Count, March 2026
Sales Assistants (573,153) lead by a wide margin. Registered Nurses (371,889) and Aged & Disabled Carers (364,661) rank second and third, reflecting Australia's growing aged care needs.
Occupation Mix by State: Share of Job Seekers per Group (%)
Each cell shows the percentage of a state's total job seekers in that occupation group. The ACT has 32.9% Professionals — reflecting Canberra's public-sector economy. WA leads in Technicians & Trades (16.3%) due to its resources industry.
Part Three

The Divide: Where Pressure Outpaces Population

The national average is 530 job seekers per 1,000 people, but regions range from 411 to 661. Every single SA4 region worsened between 2019 and 2025.

Deviation from National Average: Job Seekers per 1,000 People by Region
Each dot is one SA4 region. The dashed line marks the national average (530). Derived by combining ABS population data with DEWR job seeker counts. Hover to identify any region.
Job Seeker Growth by State: 2019 vs 2025
Each line connects a state's 2019 and 2025 annual totals. Every state increased. WA grew most at 20.6%, followed by QLD at 18.6%.
Population vs Job Seekers by SA4 Region
Each point is one SA4 region, coloured by state. Dashed lines mark median values creating four quadrants. Upper-left — small population, high job seekers — represents the most strained regions. Hover for details.
Top 20 Regions: Largest Increase in Job Seekers per 1,000 People, 2019 to 2025
The 20 regions with the biggest worsening. Grey dot = 2019; red dot = 2025. Brisbane Inner City and Perth Inner saw the largest rises — over 120 additional job seekers per 1,000 people in six years.

💡 Australia's jobs divide is deepening everywhere. Inner-city regions also recorded the sharpest increases in job-seeking pressure — population growth and job-seeking demand are rising together in the same dense places.
