Part One

Where Australians Live

Australia's population clusters almost entirely along the eastern seaboard and south-western corner. New South Wales and Victoria alone are home to more than half of all Australians, while the vast interior remains sparsely populated.

Population by State, June 2025
Source: ABS Regional Population Growth, 2024–25. NSW leads with 8.6 million residents. The NT is home to fewer than 265,000 people — less than 1% of the national total.
Annual Population Growth by State, 2024–25
Western Australia grew fastest at 2.2%. Tasmania recorded the slowest growth at 0.3%.
Components of Population Change by State
Three ABS columns — natural increase, net internal migration, and net overseas migration — combined into one stacked bar per state. QLD and WA are the only states gaining residents internally.

💡 NSW loses around 24,000 residents per year to other states, yet still grows — powered by over 90,000 overseas arrivals annually. Queensland gains from both internal migration and overseas arrivals.

Part Two

Australia's Employment Landscape

Employment counts have grown from 11.9 million in 2015 to 14.6 million in 2026 — a rise of 22.5%. The pressure is distributed unevenly across regions, occupations, and states.

Employed Workers per 1,000 Residents by SA4 Region
Each SA4 region is shaded by the number of employed workers per 1,000 residents. Darker red indicates higher employment concentration. Boundary data from ABS SA4 Digital Boundary Files, ASGS Edition 3 (2021). Hover over any region for details.
National Employed Workers, September 2015 – March 2026
Employment counts plateaued briefly before the COVID-19 period (shaded), then grew from 2021 onwards.
Employment Trend by State, 2015–2025
Five states shown on a shared Y-axis scale for direct comparison. Queensland's steep upward trend reflects both population growth and its expanding labour market.
Top 15 Occupations by Employment Count, March 2026
Sales Assistants lead with 573,153 workers. Registered Nurses (371,889) and Aged & Disabled Carers (364,661) rank second and third, reflecting Australia's growing aged care workforce.
Occupation Mix by State: Share of Workers per Group (%)
Each cell shows what percentage of each state's total employed workers fall in that occupation group. The ACT has 32.9% Professionals — reflecting Canberra's public-sector economy. WA leads in Technicians & Trades (16.3%) due to its resources industry.
Part Three

The Divide: Where Employment Concentration Differs

The national average is 530 employed workers per 1,000 residents, but individual regions range from 411 to 661 — a difference of 250 workers per 1,000 people between the most and least employed regions. When population data and employment counts are combined, a clear geographic pattern emerges: employment growth since 2019 has been fastest in inner-city areas, not in regional or remote Australia.

Deviation from National Average: Workers per 1,000 Residents by Region
Each dot is one SA4 region. The dashed line marks the national average (530 workers per 1,000 residents). Derived by combining ABS population data with employment counts. Hover to identify any region.
Employment Growth by State: 2019 vs 2025
Each line connects a state's 2019 and 2025 annual employment totals. Every state grew. WA grew most at 20.6%, followed by QLD at 18.6%.
Population vs Employed Workers by SA4 Region
Each point is one SA4 region, coloured by state. Dashed lines mark median values creating four quadrants. Upper-left — small population, high employed workers — represents the most strained regions. Hover for details.
Top 20 Regions: Largest Increase in Workers per 1,000 Residents, 2019 to 2025
The 20 SA4 regions with the largest growth in employed workers per 1,000 residents since 2019. Grey dot = 2019; red dot = 2025. Brisbane Inner City and Perth Inner grew most — over 120 additional workers per 1,000 residents in six years.

💡 Australia's employment divide is widening. Inner-city regions recorded the sharpest increases in employment concentration — population growth and employment are rising together in the same dense places.
