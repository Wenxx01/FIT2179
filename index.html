Australia's
Jobs Divide

Where do Australians live — and where is the pressure to find work most intense? This visualisation maps the gap between population and job-seeker concentration across all 88 regions of Australia, revealing a nation of sharp contrasts between growing cities, ageing coastal towns, and remote communities.

Part One

Where Australians Live

Australia's 27.6 million people cluster almost entirely along the eastern and south-western coastlines. NSW and Victoria alone account for over 57% of the population. Understanding this geography is essential context for the jobs divide.

Chart 1 — Population
Population by State, June 2025
Source: ABS Regional Population Growth 2024–25. NSW leads with 8.6 million people. The NT has fewer than 265,000 residents — under 1% of the national total.
Chart 2 — Growth Rate
Population Growth 2024–25 (%)
WA grew fastest at 2.2%, driven by overseas migration. Tasmania barely grew at 0.3%.
Chart 3 — Custom: Migration Components
What Is Driving Population Change?
Custom-built: Three ABS columns (natural increase, internal migration, overseas migration) combined using a fold transform into one diverging stacked bar per state. QLD and WA are the only states gaining residents from within Australia.

💡 Key insight: NSW loses 24,000 residents per year to interstate moves, but offsets this with 90,000 overseas arrivals. Queensland gains people from both within Australia and overseas — the fastest-growing big state.

Part Two

The Search for Work

Job seeker registrations have climbed from 11.9 million in 2015 to 14.6 million in 2026. Growth paused during COVID-19 but then accelerated. The pressure is not felt equally across occupations or regions.

Part Three

The Divide: Mismatch Between People and Pressure

Combining both datasets reveals a clear and deepening divide. Some regions carry far more job-seeking pressure per resident than others — and this has worsened in every single region since 2019. The national average is 530 job seekers per 1,000 people, but individual regions range from 411 to 661.

Chart 9 — Custom: Diverging Dot Plot
Job Seekers per 1,000 People — Deviation from National Average (530)
Custom-built: Each dot is one SA4 region plotted by how far above or below the national average its job seeker rate sits. Red = above average; teal = below. The deviation is a derived column calculated by subtracting 530 from each region's JS/1,000 figure — combining both data sources. Hover for region names.
Chart 10 — Custom: Slope Chart
Job Seekers by State: 2019 versus 2025
Custom-built: A slope chart connects two time points with one line per state — a non-standard idiom making change direction and magnitude immediately visible. Every state increased. QLD grew 18.6%; WA grew 20.6%.
Chart 11 — Custom: Quadrant Scatter
Population vs Job Seekers per SA4 Region — Four Quadrants
Custom-built: Each point is an SA4 region. Dashed lines at median values create four annotated quadrants. Top-left (small population, high job seekers) = regions under the most strain per resident. Coloured by state; hover for details. Derived from both data sources combined.
Chart 12 — Custom: Dumbbell Chart
Change in Job Seekers per 1,000 People: 2019 → 2025, All 88 SA4 Regions
Custom-built: Each row = one SA4 region. Grey dot = 2019; red dot = 2025; connecting line shows change. Sorted by 2025 value. Not a single region improved — every SA4 in Australia recorded a higher job seeker rate in 2025 than 2019. Requires derived ratios from both data sources combined.

💡 Conclusion: Australia's jobs divide is deepening everywhere. Every region had more job seekers per resident in 2025 than in 2019. The pressure is concentrated in inner-city areas, suggesting that population growth and job-seeking demand rise together in the densest places, intensifying competition for work where people are most concentrated.
